## What could test an SNN implementation benefit

The remaining hypothesis is **lower complete deployment work at equally useful predictions**, compared with an equally event-driven scalar implementation. The audit implements an eleven-dimension work ledger with original JSON-pointer provenance, units and arm/phase scope. It keeps counters separate instead of assigning arbitrary conversion weights.

| Required dimension | What the selected evidence can establish | Required closure |
| --- | --- | --- |

No selected receipt closes all stages. In particular, appliance CPU **includes reference and instrumentation**, so it is retained under that scope rather than relabeled deployment CPU. Some receipts provide only routes and queue peaks; no byte or total-queue-work estimate is invented. The combined complete-accounting flag remains false. CPU-work claims and physical-energy claims need their respective measurements; lack of energy measurement alone is not a claim that CPU work cannot be compared.

A useful-system benefit must still pass source, causal, quality, state and actual-write gates with strong current, sufficient history, scalar, timing-ablation and outcome controls. The scalar comparator gets the same native events, causal information and receipt/expiry scheduler; forcing it to poll a dense clock would create an artificial advantage. A bounded accounting feasibility check can validate these definitions using existing synthetic witnesses, but cannot itself establish independent usefulness or authorize R3 optimization. The earlier project's runtime studies remain governed by their own evidence and are outside this receipt roster.
