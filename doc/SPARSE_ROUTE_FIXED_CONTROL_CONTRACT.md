Every role starts from the same initial state within its factor/engine cell. All continue learning the same nine externally scheduled routes per receipt, including routes outside the shadow pool. Permanent means **permanent shadow-pool membership**, not permanent learning allocation. At the initial selection before receipt 180, it selects its own three lowest `(score, route_id)` routes per output. It never triggers, explores or commits again. Its output winner within that frozen pool may still change as scores change. It is not the old adaptive permanent-learning arm.

Only shadow predictions may drive their own detector/lifecycle; neither primary nor shadow selection can alter training addresses or primary readout. Errors are route-local residuals, not main-readout losses. Candidate and permanent therefore have identical true-label receipts and local count/observation/error/weight core trajectories, although their selector metadata can differ. This is a conditional mathematical implication of the specified implementation, **not a measured equality of future engines**.

Consequently, candidate-minus-permanent primary tail accuracy is **structurally zero**; the primary stationary difference is also zero. A nonzero future primary gap would indicate a broken matching/isolation implementation. The original +0.10 gain-over-permanent gate cannot be established by that primary channel. Receipt `t` still owns the one pending item and receives its credit at **t+1**. `pi(t)` is source-label provenance, not the pending receipt ID or timestamp. That distinction prevents a shuffled source index from moving an outcome's dispatch time or invalidating identity checks. Duplicates, wrong identity, early/late labels and invalid payloads remain reject-before-mutation runtime requirements from the parent contract.

This is an **offline negative control**: a source index can refer to a later label in the same phase.
